Riverside's older lots back onto the floodplain, and that shapes the tree work here. Fast-growing cottonwoods and silver maples get big quickly and drop limbs readily. Saturated soil after high water can loosen root plates on trees that looked stable a week earlier.

## Tree problems common in Riverside

- **Large, brittle species**: cottonwoods and silver maples are prone to limb failure in wind and ice.
- **Saturated soils**: after flooding, check for new leans, cracked soil or lifted roots before the next storm.
- **Narrow side yards**: many lots need equipment that fits through a gate, or hand-carried brush.

## Before you schedule work in Riverside

- Trees between the sidewalk and the street may be city-owned. Check with the city before you cut.
- If a tree is touching service lines to your house, call the electric utility first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are cottonwoods worth keeping?

A healthy, well-pruned cottonwood far from structures can be fine. Near a house, its size and limb-drop habit often make removal the safer long-term choice. Get an on-site assessment.

### Does high water damage trees?

Prolonged flooding can starve roots of oxygen and soften the soil holding the root plate. Watch for leaning, canopy thinning or dieback in the season after a flood.
