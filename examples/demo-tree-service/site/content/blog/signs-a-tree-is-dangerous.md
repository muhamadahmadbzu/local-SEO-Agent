Most tree failures give warnings first. Walk your yard after every major storm and once each season, and look for these signs.

[[toc]]

## 1. Dead branches

Dead limbs, sometimes called "widowmakers", can fall without wind. Look for branches with no leaves in season or with bark peeling away.

## 2. Cracks and splits

Deep vertical cracks in the trunk, or a crack where two large stems meet, are serious structural defects.

## 3. Fungus at the base

Mushrooms or shelf fungi on the trunk or root flare can signal internal decay. The tree may look healthy on top while its base is rotting.

## 4. A new or worsening lean

A lean that has been there for decades is often stable. A new lean, especially with cracked or raised soil on the opposite side, is an emergency.

## 5. Root damage

Roots cut by trenching, driveways or construction can kill a tree slowly, or loosen its anchor.

## 6. Thinning canopy

Sparse leaves, early fall color or dieback at the top can show stress, disease or root problems.

## 7. Cavities and hollow sections

Not every hollow tree is dangerous. Large cavities, or ones near the base, deserve a professional look.

## What to do next

If the tree threatens a structure, people or power lines, keep everyone clear and call for help. For power lines, call the electric utility first. For everything else, an on-site assessment is the next step.
