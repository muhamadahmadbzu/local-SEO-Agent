A stump left after a removal is a trip hazard, a mower obstacle and, for some species, a source of persistent sprouts. Grinding chews the stump into wood chips below ground level so the spot can be filled and planted.

## How stump grinding works

A grinder with a toothed cutting wheel sweeps across the stump and shaves it down into chips. Most residential grinding goes several inches below grade, which is enough for sod or seed. Deeper grinding takes more time and is worth asking about if you plan to plant a new tree in the same spot.

## Before the grinder arrives

- **Call 811** so underground utilities can be marked. Grinding goes below ground level.
- **Point out irrigation lines and invisible pet fences.** They are not marked by 811.
- **Decide about chips.** You can keep them as mulch, or ask for haul-away and backfill with soil.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will the roots keep growing after grinding?

The remaining roots of most species decay over time. Some species can sprout from roots. Those sprouts can be mowed or cut, or the provider can suggest options.

### Can I plant a new tree where the stump was?

It's better to plant a few feet away, or to grind deeper and replace the chip-filled soil, because decaying wood ties up nitrogen as it breaks down.
