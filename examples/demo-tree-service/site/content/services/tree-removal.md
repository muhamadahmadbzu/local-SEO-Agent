Removing a mature tree is controlled demolition. Done well, the tree comes down in pieces, each one lowered on ropes where it can't hit anything. Done badly, it ends in a damaged roof or an injury. Here is what a professional removal in Demo City should look like.

## When a tree should come out

- **Dead or dying**: no leaves in season, peeling bark, fungus at the base or large dead limbs.
- **Leaning toward a target**: a new or worsening lean toward your house, especially with cracked or lifted soil on the other side.
- **Structural defects**: split trunks, cavities or co-dominant stems with a crack where they meet.
- **Root problems**: roots cut by construction or soil heaving after storms.
- **Wrong tree, wrong place**: trees growing into foundations, sewer lines or overhead wires.

If you are unsure, ask for an assessment before you decide. Some trees can be saved with pruning or cabling.

## How a removal is done

1. **Site assessment**: drop zones, rigging points, power lines and access for chippers and trucks.
2. **Limb removal**: a climber or bucket truck removes limbs from the top down and rigs heavy pieces to the ground.
3. **Trunk sections**: the trunk is taken down in manageable sections, or felled whole when there is room.
4. **Cleanup**: brush is chipped, wood is cut and either hauled away or left for firewood, as you agreed.
5. **Stump**: left at ground level unless [stump grinding](/services/stump-grinding/) is part of the job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a tree removal take?

Most residential removals take a few hours to a full day. Large trees over structures, or jobs that need a crane, can take longer.

### Can you remove a tree that is near power lines?

Work near energized lines is restricted to qualified line-clearance professionals. Call your electric utility first. Many utilities will make the line safe or remove limbs touching their lines.

### Will removal damage my lawn?

Heavy equipment can leave ruts, especially on wet ground. Ask the provider how they protect turf, for example with ground mats, and how they handle cleanup.
